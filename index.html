Read the activities, not just the keywords

SWMFY27: compare your proposed activities with the notice

“Rural” and “nonprofit” are starting points, not a funding match. These three fictional cases show how a proposed use of funds can change the answer—even when the organization’s label stays the same.

Compare the three casesBlank screening tableOfficial sources

Independent and not affiliated with the U.S. government. This manual example uses the FY2027 Solid Waste Management Grant Program notice, SWMFY27, published October 1, 2026 and accessed October 23, 2026.

This is a purpose comparison, not a comprehensive eligibility review, legal advice, application review or award prediction. Later amendments are not covered. Check the current official notice and guidance before relying on any requirement.

01 /

Same organization label.
Three different proposals.

Every case below is invented. No real organization, applicant or town is being assessed.

Shared fictional profile: “Example Rural Youth Nonprofit,” with a mission of rural youth education and wellbeing.

Proposed geography: a fictional Iowa town with 4,800 residents. Exact address and service area are unknown; geographic eligibility is unverified.

Matching resources: none available in any case.

Case A

Music lessons & food aid

Purpose mismatch

Funded activities: music lessons and food parcels only; no landfill or solid-waste technical assistance or training.

The stated proposal does not address the notice’s listed solid-waste purposes. Shared rural status does not make it a match.

Next handling: do not recommend this proposal for this notice. This is not a judgment about every possible project by a youth organization.

NOFO §1.2, p. 4; §3.3, p. 7.

Case B

Landfill-operator training

Purpose plausibly aligned only

Funded activities: free training for new adult landfill operators on active-landfill operation and maintenance skills, plus site planning for financial and operational sustainability. No purchase, construction or facility operation costs are proposed.

These activities correspond to listed purposes. The youth-organization label alone does not rule them out.

Next handling: identify missing authority, capacity and project evidence. Compatibility with the organization’s mission and legal authority remains unknown. This is not an eligibility finding or recommendation to apply.

NOFO §1.2(ii) and its accompanying demonstration requirements, p. 4; §3.3, p. 7.

Case C

Activities not specified

Purpose unknown

Funded activities: unknown. Only the organization’s name and broad mission have been supplied.

A label cannot stand in for a project description. There is not enough information to compare the requested use of funds.

Next handling: describe the actual funded activities before assessing purpose fit. Do not fill the gap with assumptions.

NOFO §1.2, p. 4; §3.3, p. 7.

No matching funds does not disqualify these cases.

For this notice, §3.2 (p. 7) states that cost sharing or matching is not required; voluntary contributions are considered in review. That does not establish a competitive score, adequate delivery resources or eligibility.

Unknown in all three cases: legal status, legal authority, capacity, experience, detailed budget, registration and compliance evidence. Overall eligibility and readiness are not established. NOFO §3.1 (pp. 5–6) addresses applicant requirements; §§4.2–4.3 (pp. 7–8) address application materials and registration. §1.4 (p. 5) incorporates geographic definitions this example does not independently resolve.
